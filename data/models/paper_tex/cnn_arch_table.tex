\paragraph{Benchmark CNNs.}
We evaluate on 24 convolutional networks (Table~\ref{tab:cnn-arch}): six depths
$d \in \{1,\dots,6\}$ (number of convolutional layers) times four variants, defined by two choices.
(i)~\emph{Downsampling}: stride-2 convolutions (\textsf{S}$d$) or $2{\times}2$ average pooling
(\textsf{AB}$d$).
(ii)~\emph{Width}: wide (8/16/32 channels) or narrow (prefix \textsf{N}, 4/8/16 channels).
In the pooled variants the ReLU is applied \emph{before} the pool, so \textsf{AB}$d$ has exactly
as many ReLU neurons as its twin \textsf{S}$d$. The pair differs only in how it downsamples, and
since average pooling is affine it adds no case splits to the SMT encoding.
Batch normalisation is used in training and folded into the preceding convolution at export.
For example, \textsf{S3} is $\cs{8}\to\cs{16}\to\cv{16}\to\text{FC}_{32}\to\text{FC}_K$. On MNIST
its feature maps are $8{\times}14{\times}14$, $16{\times}7{\times}7$ and $16{\times}7{\times}7$,
giving $1568+784+784+32 = 3168$ ReLUs.
